04 · The shifter

A switch is three writes. Here they are.

No daemon, no shim, no rewriting of Claude Code. Sidestep edits the three places your login actually lives and gets out of the way. In this order.

1Snapshot

The login that's leaving gets filed to disk under its own email, so it's still there when you want it back.

$ write ~/.cli-proxy-api/
    claude-john@sidestep.sh.json
access + refresh + expiry, 0600
→
2Swap

The account you picked gets written into the one Keychain item Claude Code reads. Same item, new tokens.

$ security add-generic-password \
  -s "Claude Code-credentials" \
  -a john -U
→
3Point

One field in your config says which account is yours. It gets updated, and the old file is kept next to it.

~/.claude.json · bak .sidestep-bak
- "oauthAccount": john@sidestep.sh
+ "oauthAccount": derek@sidestep.sh

Claude Code doesn't hold your token in memory. Every turn it shells out to security find-generic-password and caches the answer for 30 seconds. So the session you left running in the other window picks up the new account on its next turn, all by itself. Nothing restarts. No relaunch, no "please quit and reopen", no lost context.

04 · The shifter

The gate that won't open.

Refresh tokens rot. Sign in somewhere else, let another tool rotate it, leave it alone long enough, and Anthropic stops taking it. Most apps find out at the worst possible moment, halfway through a request. Sidestep finds out on the next poll and tells you before you've committed to anything.

  • The rowsGo to dashes. No made-up zero, no stale number from an hour ago. It doesn't know, so it doesn't say.
  • The noteNames who the token actually belongs to, because half the time you signed in as the wrong person in a browser you forgot was logged in.
  • The gateGoes hatched, and the knob refuses it. You cannot switch onto a broken login by accident.
  • The fixHit SIGN IN, or + ADD ACCOUNT. Real OAuth, real browser, and the fresh token gets written to the proxy copy too, so nothing goes stale behind your back again.

Percentages tell you where you are. The tape tells you when it changes. Every account's 7-day window, drawn against the calendar, with NOW down the middle.

Each row

One bar per account. It fills as you spend.

The bar runs from when the weekly window opened to when it resets. The label on the end tick is the only number you need: how much is gone, and when it comes back. The thin orange line underneath is the Fable bucket on the same clock.

Picking one

Switch to whichever resets soonest.

Everything right of NOW is still coming. Grab the account whose tick lands first. The ◆ is the 5-hour session reset, usually tonight. Locked accounts show NO DATA · SIGN IN rather than a guess.

05 · Field manual · reset windows

Read it in three seconds.

Five marks. That's the entire notation. Once you've seen it you can't unsee it, which is the point of a chart.

Plate 1 · the tape as drawn
OPENRESET

Bar length = 7-day window

Starts when your weekly quota opened. Ends at the tick where it resets. Always seven days wide, wherever it sits on the calendar.

Fill = used

Grey under 50, amber to 79, red from 80. The label at the tick says the number and the day: "70% SUN 2P".

Thin orange = the Fable bucket

Fable has its own weekly limit, separate from the 7D row. Same clock, thinner line, so it never gets confused with the weekly.

◆ = 5H reset

The session window. It's a point, not a bar, because five hours is a blink at this scale. Usually the thing you're actually waiting on.

NOW

Orange line = NOW

Dead center, always. Left is spent. Right is coming. Whatever tick is nearest on the right is your next switch.

It refreshes every five minutes with the rest of the panel, so the ticks drift left in real time. Watch one cross NOW and you'll see the bar empty out. Cheap thrills, but ours.
